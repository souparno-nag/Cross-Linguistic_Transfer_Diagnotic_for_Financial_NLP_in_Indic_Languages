PRESENTATION GUIDE  (15 minutes, read-aloud notes)
Cross-Linguistic Transfer Diagnostic for Financial NLP in Indic Languages

How to use this: each section has a time budget, what to say (plain words, say it
your own way), and one number to land. If you run long, cut the lines marked [CUT].
Total: about 10 sections, ~1.5 minutes each.

======================================================================
1. OPENING                                              0:00 - 1:00
======================================================================
SAY:
- "A model trained on Hindi financial text is used on Bengali or Telugu with no
  retraining. It gets much worse. My project asks: how much worse, and WHY?"
- "Most work stops at 'it got worse'. I built a pipeline that tries to name a cause
  for each individual failure."

LAND: two questions - how much is lost, and what is it lost to.

======================================================================
2. THE PROBLEM                                          1:00 - 2:30
======================================================================
SAY:
- Languages: Hindi, Bengali (Indo-Aryan); Telugu, Malayalam (Dravidian). Different
  scripts, different word structure.
- Zero-shot transfer = train on one language, test on another, zero examples of the
  second.
- The hard part is fairness: if Hindi and Bengali test sets contain different
  sentences, you cannot tell if the drop is language or just a harder test set.
- My answer: build a corpus where the SAME sentence exists in every language.

LAND: "same content, only the language changes."

======================================================================
3. THE CORPUS (Phase 1)                                 2:30 - 4:30
======================================================================
SAY:
- Source data: IndicFinNLP - Hindi, Bengali, Telugu. Three tasks: numerals in
  financial text (task 1), sustainable vs not (task 2, binary), ESG headlines
  (task 3, 10 classes).
- No Malayalam upstream, so I machine-translated into it. Used IndicTrans2 (1B model),
  direct Indic-to-Indic, never through English. 9 translation directions.
- Frozen, immutable release: about 123,700 rows over 36 splits, Parquet only,
  checksummed.
- Quality gate: LaBSE similarity between source and translation. Threshold 0.82 for
  task 2; I recalibrated task 3 to 0.73 because short headlines score lower even for
  human translations. Low-scoring rows are flagged, never deleted.
- Early surprise: tasks 2 and 3 are already parallel across languages (the same
  sentences, shuffled). I had to align them before anything else.

LAND: 1 in 6 task-1 numerals (17.2%) did not survive translation intact -
financial numbers are fragile.
[CUT] the alignment details and the two translation corruption modes.

======================================================================
4. THE MODELS (Phase 2)                                 4:30 - 6:00
======================================================================
SAY:
- Classifier = pretrained encoder + [CLS] vector + one linear layer. Deliberately
  minimal so nothing but the encoder can explain differences.
- Two encoders: IndicBERT-v2 (34M parameters, Indic-specialised) and mBERT (179M,
  broad multilingual). XLM-R did not fit on my 4 GB GPU - state this honestly.
- Three seeds everywhere, config hashes, resumable training.
- In-language results (held-out Hindi): task 2 macro-F1 0.82 (IndicBERT) vs 0.87
  (mBERT); task 3 is much harder: 0.15 vs 0.34.

LAND: mBERT beats IndicBERT-v2 even in-language - a first hint that specialised is
not automatically better.
[CUT] training details (AdamW, warmup, fp16).

======================================================================
5. ZERO-SHOT TRANSFER (Phase 3)                         6:00 - 8:00
======================================================================
SAY:
- Frozen model, eval mode, no gradient - enforced by assertions in the code.
- Transfer gap = score in source language minus score in target language, on the
  same items. 9 cells, 3 seeds each, with bootstrap confidence intervals.
- Task 2 (IndicBERT-v2): about 0.95 macro-F1 at home falls to 0.50-0.70 elsewhere -
  often close to guessing. Gaps of about 0.26 to 0.45.
- Best cell: Bengali to Hindi. Worst: Hindi to Bengali/Telugu.
- Translationese test: the same Hindi model on native Hindi vs machine-translated
  Hindi loses 6-7 points - translation alone costs something.
- Task 3 is low everywhere; some Telugu-source gaps are within seed noise, so I say
  so rather than claim an effect.

LAND: transfer is far from free, and not symmetric: into Dravidian costs more than
back out.
NOTE if asked: the 0.95 "home" score is measured on the paired items, not the
held-out fold - that is why it differs from the baselines in section 4.

======================================================================
6. WHICH SOURCE, WHICH ENCODER (Phases 4-5)             8:00 - 9:30
======================================================================
SAY:
- I trained Bengali and Telugu sources too, so every language is tried as a source.
- Hindi turned out to be the WORST source on both encoders - the obvious choice is
  not the best one.
- Does language-family closeness predict the best source? Mixed: yes in some
  cells, no in others. Do not overclaim.
- mBERT beat IndicBERT-v2 on every measure. But mBERT is also about 5x bigger, so I
  cannot separate "more languages" from "more parameters". That test did not fit
  the hardware.

LAND: an honest "not yet known" is a result.

======================================================================
7. THE DIAGNOSTIC PIPELINE (Phase 6)                    9:30 - 12:00
======================================================================
SAY:
- Start from a failure: the model got this sentence RIGHT in the source language and
  WRONG in the target. That rules out "just a hard item".
- Stage 1, the gate: is the translation still faithful (LaBSE below threshold)? If
  not, label it translation drift and stop. Order matters, or my own translation
  noise would be blamed on the language.
- Stage 2, linguistic checks: tokenizer fragmentation (text chopped into 2x more
  pieces), morphological masking (key term hidden inside a suffixed word), and
  terminology gap (Integrated Gradients: does the model look at the key term as much
  in the target?).
- Stage 3, data and model checks: fails in all three target languages? right on
  another seed? run predicts one class for everything? right on the machine vs
  native twin text?
- Exactly one label per failure by a fixed priority, plus a full audit trail. If
  nothing fires: "unattributed" - reported openly.
- Rules: no tuning thresholds to get a tidy chart; thresholds come from a null
  distribution of correct pairs.

LAND: "every failure gets one label and a receipt."
[CUT] Integrated Gradients details; just say "attention attribution".

======================================================================
8. FINDINGS (Phase 7)                                   12:00 - 13:30
======================================================================
SAY:
- Share of failures by group, across 4 task/encoder cells:
    data labels ........ 45% - 72%   (mostly: the item fails in every target language)
    model labels ....... 7% - 34%    (seed instability, class collapse)
    linguistic ......... 9% - 12%
    unattributed ....... 9% - 20%
- Meaning: most failures are described by WHERE they happen, not by a property of the
  target sentence. Rule-based linguistic causes explain only about a tenth.
- A difference counts only if confidence intervals do not overlap AND it points the
  same way on both encoders. Under that strict rule, few comparisons survive: e.g.
  Telugu-source runs show more model-instability labels than Hindi-source on task 2;
  most other comparisons are within noise.

LAND: the pipeline works as a tool, and the honest headline is "not mainly a
tokenizer or morphology problem".

======================================================================
9. LIMITATIONS (say these before someone asks)          13:30 - 14:30
======================================================================
- Morphological masking and terminology gap are labelled as causes, but my own
  validity checks show they do not predict failure (masked terms do not fail more;
  attribution fires at about chance). Reported as limitations next to the result.
- Data and model labels are descriptive: they say where, not why.
- Hand check: 197/200 agreement, but only task 2 IndicBERT, and only 40 of the 200
  rows were linguistic labels.
- No native Malayalam. Translation quality was sample-checked (about 180 rows per
  task), not fully audited by native speakers.
- Same sentences across languages is deliberate: results mean "language changes,
  content fixed", not "works on unseen text".
- 4 GB GPU: no XLM-R; task 3 is at its data ceiling.

======================================================================
10. CLOSE                                               14:30 - 15:00
======================================================================
SAY:
- Built: a frozen parallel corpus, a controlled transfer measurement, and a
  diagnostic pipeline with audit trail.
- Learned: transfer loses a lot; Hindi is not the best source; a bigger general
  encoder beat the Indic one; most failures are not explained by simple linguistic
  rules.
- Next: a controlled test of capacity vs multilinguality, better validity for the
  morphology and terminology checks.
- "Thank you - happy to take questions."

======================================================================
LIKELY QUESTIONS (short answers)
======================================================================
Q: Isn't testing on translated versions of the training sentences a leak?
A: It is the design. I want to hold content fixed so only the language changes. It
   measures the cost of switching language, not generalisation to new text.

Q: Why trust machine-translated Malayalam?
A: I don't fully - it is flagged as a limitation. Quality was checked by LaBSE on
   every pair and by hand on a sample, and Malayalam has no native reference.

Q: Why only two encoders?
A: XLM-R needs 4.14 GiB just for weights and optimiser state; my GPU has 3.68 GiB.

Q: What does a gap of 0.45 mean?
A: Macro-F1 falls from about 0.95 to about 0.50 - near guessing for a two-class task.

Q: How do you know a label is right?
A: Data/model labels are read directly from logs. Linguistic labels are rule-based
   with known weak validity; I report that rather than hide it.

Q: Why does "unattributed" matter?
A: It is the honest measure of coverage. A pipeline explaining 100% of failures
   would be suspicious.
